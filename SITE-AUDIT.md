## Findings

1. **The first screen does not quickly explain the whole service.** A large ebook poster dominates the homepage before the email entry and offers. The replacement starts with a succinct promise and a useful next step, then explains the full system.
2. **Navigation is missing from the rendered homepage.** The page has no visible route directly to the process, resources, or offers. The replacement supplies desktop and phone navigation, section links, and footer navigation.
3. **The lead magnet lacks a clear hierarchy.** The original repeats the cover and download pitch across the page, and the top email entry has no accessible label exposed in the audit. The replacement gives the guide one substantial section, with contextual entry points from the hero, field notes, and footer CTA. Signup remains on the existing official form.
4. **The service cards don't provide a complete next step.** Only Strategy Call has an actionable booking destination in the original. Training and Premier now offer email inquiries; prices remain unspecified because none were verified.
5. **The system is described, but difficult to explore.** Dense, centered paragraphs combine ingredient work, habits, equipment, and portability. The replacement breaks these into four interactive topics and a three-step process.
6. **Imagery is inconsistent.** The live page mixes kitchen backgrounds, a tall ebook cover, a white-background cooler, a monochrome fridge photo, and a standalone YouTube graphic. The replacement uses a consistent dark hero, standardized image crops, and the original cover and relevant equipment photography in context.
7. **Important content is buried.** Useful details about sourcing, logistics, portable systems, and entertainment-industry constraints appear well below the lead magnet. These now shape the main page narrative.
8. **Accessibility needs attention.** The original images have empty alt text and the displayed headings begin at h3/h4. The replacement includes one h1, meaningful heading levels, image alternatives, a skip link, keyboard-operated tabs, focus styles, native dialogs, FAQ disclosures, and reduced-motion support.
9. **The existing metadata is basic.** The original has a descriptive title and description but an empty social image. The replacement supplies new metadata, a favicon, and a local social-image reference. The final absolute social image URL should be set after a hosting destination is approved.
10. **Some original health claims are broader than the available evidence.** The homepage and cover mention gut-health benefits and terms such as “bad oils.” The redesign preserves the actual cover as an existing asset while avoiding new health promises in the page copy. It focuses on flavor, ingredients, convenience, and repeatable systems.

## Replacement structure and funnel

1. Cinematic introduction matching the supplied reference.
2. Four-part interactive food-system explanation.
3. Existing free dressing/condiment ebook.
4. Four new practical field notes, each with a route to the free guide.
5. The $149 Strategy Call, then Training and Premier support.
6. Origin/process explanation, useful FAQs, and a final guide CTA.

The field notes are newly written educational website copy, not claims that these exact articles or videos already existed. Images of meals are illustrative, generated assets; they are not presented as customer results or a specific recipe included in the ebook.

## Before replacing the live site

- Obtain explicit approval for a hosting destination and live-domain deployment.
- Keep the original signup flow available, or supply a supported email-platform form/embed and verify delivery with a real test signup before switching the live domain. The current preview uses the original homepage opt-in; replacing that same homepage without migrating the form would make this handoff self-referential.
- Preserve the existing confirmation page and associated email automation during migration.
- Confirm whether the intake form remains the intended booking path. A calendar or payment integration requires an actual approved destination; none has been invented.
- Set the final absolute social-image URL and any canonical URL after the destination is known.
- Replace preview-specific privacy text with the business's approved policy if analytics or new data collection is added.
- Confirm any future pricing or scope changes for Training and Premier before publishing them.

No deployment or live-site change was performed.
